The problem

Thai creators own copyright the moment they make something, but registering it, finding a lawyer who actually handles copyright and acting on a copy are slow and full of legal terms.

What I did

Led UX/UI for our hackathon team, from interviews and wireframes to the pitch prototype. After the event I redesigned every screen to the standard I wanted.

Result

Top 20 at Chula LegalTech Year 6. The screens on this page are the post-hackathon redesign.

01 · Problem

Copyright is automatic. Proving it isn’t.

Under Thai law a work is protected as soon as it is made. The trouble starts when a creator needs to show it is theirs.

01Registration feels like a legal exam

Creators can record their work with the Department of Intellectual Property, but the forms use legal terms with no explanation.

02Lawyers are found by word of mouth

There was no way to tell which lawyers actually handle copyright, how they charge, or how fast they reply.

03Cases live in chat threads

Lawyers tracked documents and deadlines across LINE, email and paper, so clients kept asking for status.

02 · Wireframes to hi-fi

From six wireframes to one system

Three journeys: register a work, find a lawyer, and let the lawyer run the case. I wireframed each in grey first, tested the order of steps with the team, then built the hi-fi screens on one set of components.
