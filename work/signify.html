Signify

Signify👋Live

A mobile app that turns sign language into text and speech in real time, so signers and non-signers can talk without an interpreter.

RoleUX/UI designer
TypeMobile app · Accessibility
YearAug – Oct 2024
AwardCreative Award, Thai SIGCHI 2024
01 · Context

Everyday talk stalls when only one side knows sign language

The idea came from people close to me who are hearing- and speech-impaired. Ordering food, seeing a doctor or chatting in class all depend on the other person understanding them.

Signers

Deaf, hard-of-hearing and speech-impaired people who use sign language every day.

Non-signers

Staff, doctors, classmates and family who never learned it.

Learners

People who want to pick up sign language to talk with someone they know.

Cost of the gap

Conversations slow down or stop, and over time that leads to social isolation.

02 · User research

What signers and learners told us

Research focused on deaf users and people learning sign language.

They want

Fast, accurate translation

A conversation can’t wait. Translation has to keep up with signing, or people give up and switch to gestures.

Gets in the way

Mixed fluency and unfamiliar tech

Users sign at different levels, and not everyone is comfortable with apps. The UI can’t assume either.

Users suggested

A teaching module and a simpler UI

Learning tools for non-signers, and an interface with large targets, plain labels and few steps.

Key insight

The barrier isn’t one bad moment. It repeats every day, and that is what isolates people.

03 · How it works

From a sign to a spoken sentence

Gesture recognition reads the camera feed and writes each sign out as text, then reads it aloud.

01Sign in front of the camera
02Gesture recognition reads hand shape and motion
03The sentence appears in the message timeline
04Text to speech says it out loud
04 · Design decisions

One screen, built around the camera

Every part of the home screen answers a finding from research.

Mode selection
01

Let the signer choose how they are heard

Text to speech for a busy counter, text only for a quiet room. One-hand mode means the other hand can hold the phone. Both switches sit at the top so they are set before the conversation starts.

02

Give the camera the most space

Signs use hands, face and upper body. A large live view lets the signer check they are in frame instead of guessing why a sign was missed.

Message

03

Keep the conversation on screen

Each translated line stays in a timeline with the time. Tap the speaker to replay it out loud, or share it, so nobody has to ask the signer to repeat.

Camera Translation
04

One main action, always in reach

Camera Translation is the only filled button in the tab bar, at thumb height. Starting a translation is one tap from anywhere in the app.
