Project · HCI · Generative AI · Aug–Sep 2024

Communication, one gesture at a time.

Signify translates sign language into text and speech in real time, so deaf and hard-of-hearing people and non-signers can talk without an interpreter. It won the Creative Award at the Bangkok ACM SIGCHI 2024 Symposium.

Role
UX/UI designer
Type
Mobile app · HCI research
Timeline
Aug – Sep 2024
Recognition
Creative Award, ACM SIGCHI Bangkok 2024
Tools
Figma
01 Problem

Communication barriers push hearing-impaired people into isolation.

The idea came from a personal connection with people who are hearing- and speech-impaired. Everyday interactions like ordering food, asking for directions or seeing a doctor depend on someone else knowing sign language.

02 Research

Users want speed and accuracy, and a way to learn.

Need

Fast, accurate translation

Conversations can't wait for a slow app.

Challenge

Different sign fluency

Users vary in sign language comprehension and comfort with technology.

Opportunity

A teaching module

Help non-signers learn, not just translate.

A follow-up survey rated the app highly on all three key criteria. Users called out the clear interface and better communication.

03 Design

A warm palette, so it feels like a bridge, not a medical device.

I built a small design system (type scale, warm colour palette, components) so the app feels friendly and confidence-building. It's more than a translation tool.

04 Recognition

Creative Award, Bangkok ACM SIGCHI 2024.
